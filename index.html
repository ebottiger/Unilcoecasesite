Reading across the numbers

Not a funding problemOperating margin is still about 49%, debt-to-equity is 0.2, and expenses are small next to gross profit. Uniforce can afford to invest. The constraint is how people work and what they’re measured on.
Spending more to win lessSales and marketing spend rose about 9% this year (roughly +$0.45M) while the share of new customers fell. Nothing in the statements shows money going toward keeping customers.
Innovation looks flatR&D has held near $3.0M, about 2.7–2.8% of revenue, and price has been $250 for three years. That fits the CRO’s “slow innovation” point.
“Declining sales” needs a closer lookNew customers still grew 3% in two years, but churn grew faster. The bigger lever looks like retention and share, not just closing deals. Worth confirming how the CRO defines it.
Two data checksFree cash flow ($113.5M) is more than double operating profit ($53.7M), which is unusual and worth verifying. Days sales outstanding rose from 50 to 62: customers are paying more slowly. Dissatisfaction is one possible cause, not a conclusion.
